# SOUL

How the site sounds, what a visitor came for, and how the page should look. Written from the homepage. Stay under a hundred lines.

## Mission

San Diego is full of life. DEVx is the room where that life meets the technology conversation.

The homepage states the community in one line: DEVx is San Diego's developer community for makers who care about craft, side projects, and showing up for each other. The door is wide on purpose. People from all industries have something to add, whether they are using Lovable to build a website or developing leading AI harness memory infra. Everyone has something to add to the conversation.

It is free to attend and open to every skill level. The point of showing up is to meet the San Diego developer community.

## What people want

They want four things, in this order. The page meets them in this order.

### See our events

The first job is to show what is happening.

DEVx Monthly is presentations, speakers, and a social hour, every third Saturday. The afternoon is lunch at noon, a presentation at 1:30, free-form at 2:30, and a close at 4. Paper Club discusses leading AI research papers. Co-routine is weekly Friday casual co-working, moving through the cafés.

One primary button: **Our Event Calendar.** It opens the Luma calendar in this tab. The bar above the hero names the day, and its action is **RSVP Now.** Events in the nav is the way back to the dates on this site.

### Why an event is useful to them

Once the event is visible, say why the afternoon is worth it. This is copy, not a second button.

They meet people at every stage, including people from outside software. They exchange ideas in the room, over lunch and the social hour, with laptops open in the free-form block. Friday is a place to work beside someone. Paper Club is a place to read something hard together. Attendance is free.

### Learn more about us

Then they want the people who hold the room.

The homepage introduces the volunteers by name, face, and role, and makes one joke about how many developers it takes to screw in a light bulb. The scale is a group of people. Who we are is the longer page.

### Contribute to an event

Last, after they have seen an event, understood why it is useful, and learned who the room is.

The invitation is the homepage's own sentence: everyone has something to add to the conversation. The way in is the speak page. The label is **Add to the conversation.** It belongs at the close of the homepage and under the events list.

The archive is a library. Watch stays in the nav for someone who already wants a past talk. It is not an invitation, and neither is asking a new visitor to speak.

## Design

Clean, but fun. Minimal.

Clean means short lines, few sections, and one action first. Chivo carries the interface. Fraunces carries a title. The palette is paper, ink, and the purple from the wordmark. Space is part of the sentence. Items sit at least 12px apart, and 16px when the line can take it: a button and the links under it, icons in a row, cards in a grid.

Fun is one small motion or one joke, then quiet again. The wordmark sits and jumps. Portraits drift. A skyline sits behind "San Diego is full of life." A phrase gets a highlight when it is the point. Motion stops when someone prefers less of it.

A block earns its place by helping someone see an event, understand why it is useful, learn who we are, or contribute. Another color, button, or sentence needs that reason. Pop colors are seasoning.
